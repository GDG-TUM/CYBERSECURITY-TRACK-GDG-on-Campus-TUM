# 🎣 Phishing Red Flags

Phishing tricks you into giving up information or clicking something harmful. Attackers target people, not just computers.

## Check these before you act
- **Urgency or fear:** "Your account will be closed in 1 hour", "Act now"
- **The sender:** does the address or number really belong to who it claims?
- **The link:** hover or long-press to preview it. Does it match the real site?
- **The ask:** passwords, PINs, one-time codes, ID photos, payments or "fees" are big red flags
- **Too good to be true:** prizes, jobs, loans or investments with guaranteed returns
- **Odd wording or formatting:** useful, but **not reliable**
- **Unexpected attachments:** do not open them

## AI-powered scams
Scam messages can now be written in perfect English, Kiswahili or Sheng, and voices and images can be faked. **Clean writing no longer proves a message is real.** Judge the request, not the polish.

## Safer habits
- Confirm through another channel: call the person or company on a number you already know
- Never share one-time codes or PINs
- Use the official app or website you type yourself, not the link in the message
- Report suspicious messages to your provider or platform, and tell a friend who might also receive it

## If you clicked or replied
1. Do not panic, and stop interacting with the message
2. Change the password of any account involved, starting with email
3. Turn on two-factor authentication
4. Tell your bank or mobile money provider if money or a PIN was involved
5. Tell the Track Lead if it relates to the club

Practise with the [phishing detective session](../sessions/would-you-survive-a-phishing-attack.md).
