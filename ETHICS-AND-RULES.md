# ⚖️ Ethics and Rules of Engagement

The track teaches security skills that could be misused. These rules apply to **every session and lab**, and members **sign the code of conduct before any offensive-style activity**.

## The rules

1. **Attack only systems built for the purpose:** local virtual machines, CTF platforms and club-owned labs.
2. **Never test campus, personal or third-party systems** without written permission from the owner, even "just to see".
3. **Sign the code of conduct before offensive sessions.** Breaches mean removal from lab activities.
4. **Report vulnerabilities found by accident through the Lead, not publicly.** Stop, do not explore further, and tell the Track Lead, who will help you report it to the owner responsibly.
5. **Respect Kenya's Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes Act.** Unauthorised access to computers and data is a crime, and "I was only learning" is not a defence.
6. **Demonstrations of phishing or password attacks use fictional accounts and sample data only.** Never real accounts, real credential lists or real people's data.
7. **Do not share exploits, stolen data, leaked credentials or other people's private information** in public repos, chats or social media.
8. **Do not use these skills to harm, harass, cheat or steal.** This includes scams, account takeovers, SIM-swap or mobile-money fraud.
9. **Credit other people's work** and explain things in your own words.
10. **When in doubt, ask first.**

## What happens if the rules are broken
The track follows the chapter's [Code of Conduct](https://github.com/GDG-TUM/.github/blob/main/CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md). Breaking these rules can lead to removal from lab activities, the track and the organization, and serious cases may be reported to the university or other authorities.
